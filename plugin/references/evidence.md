# Evidence: how a finding is written

The walk is only worth something if a reader can check it. Three habits make
it checkable; they are the documentation's
(https://codegraph.biz/docs/discover/#three-habits-that-make-the-result-trustworthy)
and every step holds to them.

1. **Keep the kinds of knowledge apart.** A fact and a guess written in the
   same sentence become one guess.
2. **Anchor every claim.** A finding without an anchor is an opinion.
3. **Do not rank by size alone.** What is large is not what matters; what is
   large AND worked on is. Steps 1 and 3 answer that together, never step 1
   alone.

## The kinds of knowledge

Every finding carries exactly one `kind`. When it rests on several, it takes
the weakest, in this order:

| kind | What it is | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| `declared` | written in the source | an entity; an edge of provenance `declared`; a measure the extractor took from the text (`sloc`, `cyclomatic`); a count of those |
| `derived` | computed from written facts by a stated rule | an edge of provenance `derived`; a reading YOU make by a rule of the page — a break in a curve, a style, a verdict |
| `generated` | what the language's own compiler or runtime produced | an edge of provenance `generated`, present only when a trace was merged (Elixir, Ruby) |
| `dynamic-candidate` | a plausible runtime wiring, not a certainty | an edge of provenance `dynamic-candidate`: a Spring injection, a dispatch the extractor could not pin |
| `co-change` | what the commits did: how often a file changed, who changed it, which files changed together | git history (step 3), alone or joined with the model. A fact about how people work, an inference about the code |
| `explanation` | what a language model said a unit does | `codegraph explain` (step 5): `origin` is `explain`; or your own reading of the source: `origin` is `session`, and `note` says what you read |
| `said` | what a person told you | this conversation, or someone they quote |

Rules that follow:

- A report run with `--declared-only` yields `declared` findings. Without it,
  read the `provenance=` each row prints, and take the weakest.
- A ranking is as strong as its rows. "The largest type is `Basket`, 34 lines"
  is `declared`. "The size curve breaks after the third type" is your reading
  of declared numbers by the page's rule: `derived`, with the rule in `note`.
- Zero rows is not "there are none". It is "this query found none". Say which
  query, and what it cannot see for this language (`capabilities.md`).
- A number is read from the saved file, at the moment it is written down.
  Never from memory, never rounded differently in two places.
- An `explanation` always says who read the code (`origin`), and only an
  explanation does. Your own reading is not a record: nobody paid for it,
  nothing fingerprints it, and the next session cannot reuse it.
- Never upgrade a kind. A `dynamic-candidate` confirmed by a person stays one
  finding (`dynamic-candidate`), plus a second one (`said`).

## The anchor

Every finding carries one `anchor`, in one of three forms.

| Form | Fields | When |
|---|---|---|
| a place in the source | `file`, `line`, `commit` | the finding is about one entity or one edge. The model has the file and the line of every entity and edge; the commit is the step's |
| a file of the workspace | `artifact`, and `row` for one row | the finding is a table, a count or a report: cite the saved query result or the saved report, relative to the workspace |
| a person | `said` (a role, not a name), `at` | the finding is what someone told you |

A claim you cannot anchor is not written as a finding. It is written as a
question (`questions` in the state), with the step that raised it and who
could answer. A good walk ends with a list of such questions: they are what
the person takes to the team.

## The notebook entry

`NOTEBOOK.md` is the person's own record; write it so they would sign it.
One entry per step, appended, never rewritten:

```markdown
## 1 · Structure and elements — 2026-11-22, at 4f2c1a9

- **declared** — 47 types, 3 packages; 5 216 source lines. `queries/02-kinds.json`
- **derived** — the size curve breaks after `OwnerController` (the next type is
  a third of it). Rule: the first type several times smaller than the one
  before. `queries/04-largest-types.json`, rows 0-3
- **said** — "`VetRepository` is generated by the build" (the person in this
  session). Excluded from later rankings.

Open questions
- Is `PetTypeFormatter` still used? (the team that owns the web layer)
```

Lead each line with the kind, in bold. Give the anchor last. Use the person's
words for what they said, in quotes.

## What never goes in a finding

- A secret, or the value of an environment variable.
- A price. What a run cost is written to `spend`, from what the command
  reported; what the next run will cost is read from its own `--estimate`.
- A judgement of a person. Step 3 counts shares of work; it does not grade
  anyone.
